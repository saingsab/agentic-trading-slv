Silver Desk Manual

A field guide to the XAG/USD decision-support system — for whoever sits down at this desk next.

reads · computes · verifies · proposes never executes a trade

This system doesn't predict silver's price and it doesn't place orders. What it does: keep a clean, deterministic record of what the market is doing, and hold you to a fixed discipline about what you said you'd do before you did it. Everything below assumes you're sitting at a terminal in the project folder with the virtual environment active.

Before anything else

Activate the environment and make sure your FRED key is set — almost every command below depends on data that slv ingest pulls in.

# once per new terminal
$ source .venv/bin/activate
$ export FRED_API_KEY=<your key>

Step 01 · every morning

Bring the desk up to date

Three commands, run in order, every day you're paying attention to the market — whether or not you're in a trade. Nothing after this step works on stale data.

$ slv ingest    # pull prices, macro, COT, calendar (safe to re-run)
$ slv compute   # rebuild ATR, EMA, RSI, GSR, etc. from stored prices
$ slv brief     # write today's read to briefs/

slv brief writes a dated markdown file to briefs/ — open the newest one and you'll see the day's indicators, the current regime, and anything scheduled (CPI, NFP, FOMC) in the next two weeks, with the historical move each one has tended to produce.

Step 02 · the moment you see a setup

Write the thesis down — before you enter

This is the one rule the whole system exists to enforce: the record comes first. Not after you've entered, not after you've decided it worked. Before.

$ slv thesis open \
    --direction long \
    --entry 68.00-69.00 \
    --invalidation 65.80 \
    --target 74.00 \
    --size 8 \
    --leverage 3 \
    --rationale "pullback to EMA20, RSI cooling off overbought"
--directionlong or short
--entrythe price range you're planning to fill in, low–high
--invalidationthe price that proves you wrong — your stop
--size / --leverage% of equity risked, and the leverage on it
There is no edit or delete

Once a thesis is written, it's permanent. Got a number wrong? You don't fix it — you write a new one. That's deliberate: it's what makes the record trustworthy later, including to you.

Step 03 · whenever the trade actually ends

Close it out

Could be two days later, could be six weeks — whenever price actually hits your stop, your target, or you decide to get out. Record what really happened, not what you planned.

$ slv thesis close 1 --entry 68.50 --exit 71.00

--entry is what you actually paid; --exit is what you actually got. Nothing gets scored yet — this step just writes down the raw facts.

Step 04 · every Saturday

Score the week, not the P&L

One command, run weekly, grades every trade that's been closed since you last ran it — against five fixed checks, none of which care whether you made money.

$ slv journal score
thesis 1: R=+0.83 process_score=1.00 (5/5)
  • ✓ Falsifiable — you named a real price that proves you wrong
  • ✓ Sized — your position respected event risk in the holding window
  • ✓ Honored — you exited at or before your stated stop
  • ✓ In-condition — your actual fill was inside your planned zone
  • ✓ Pre-committed — the thesis existed before the trade did

A trade that loses money can still score 5/5 if you did everything right. A trade that makes money can score low if you skipped the stop or sized in blind. That gap is the entire point — it's what tells you whether you're improving, separate from whether the market cooperated.

Step 05 · before you trust an idea with size

Backtest it first

A rule is a written-down version of a setup — conditions, a stop, a target — tested automatically against years of history instead of your memory of a few recent trades.

$ slv backtest run rules/pullback_ema20_long.json
train (walk-forward, 8 folds): n=71 expectancy=-0.02R median=-0.77R stdev=1.22 range=[-1.17R, +2.00R] holdout: sealed (not evaluated) -- pass --unlock-holdout --reason '...' to touch it

Notice holdout stays sealed. The most recent fifth of history is locked away on purpose — it's the one honest check you get on whether a rule actually works, and it only stays honest if you don't peek at it every time you tweak a number. Unlocking it is meant to be rare, deliberate, and logged:

$ slv backtest run rules/pullback_ema20_long.json \
    --unlock-holdout --reason "final check before sizing up"

Optional · anytime

Ask, instead of scrolling

Two ways to ask a question and get an answer grounded in your real, computed data — not a guess.

From this terminal

$ slv agent ask "What did the last three pullback-to-EMA20 \
    theses look like, and how did they score?"

Runs a small local model in a sandboxed container — it can only read your indicators, your regime state, and your journal. It has no way to reach the sealed holdout, and no way to place a trade.

From inside Claude Code

Just ask, in plain language — "what's silver's RSI right now" or "show me my open theses." The project is already wired up to answer from your real database instead of searching the web.

When something looks wrong

Common snags

slv ingest fails with "FRED_API_KEY is not set"

Expected, not a bug — the key only lives in your current shell. Run export FRED_API_KEY=<your key> again, or add it to your shell profile so it survives new terminals.

slv journal score prints nothing

Means there's nothing new to grade — every closed thesis is already scored, or nothing's been closed since last time. Not an error.

A backtest says "not reportable"

Fewer than 20 trades came out of the walk-forward run. The system refuses to show an expectancy number built on too small a sample rather than show you something precise-looking and misleading. Loosen the rule's conditions or widen the date range and try again.

I opened a thesis just to try the command — can I undo it?

Not by editing it — that's by design. If it was only a test, the whole database can be rebuilt clean from scratch (nothing in it besides your journal and backtest history is irreplaceable):

$ rm data/slv.db
$ slv ingest && slv compute
